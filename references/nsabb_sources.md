Primary NSABB documents referenced or relevant to the casebook.

## Foundational Policy Documents

**NSABB DURC Policy (2012)**
- National Science Advisory Board for Biosecurity. (2012). *Recommendations on the Oversight of Dual Use Research of Concern.* NSABB. Washington, DC: HHS.
- Defines seven DURC categories, two-stage review process, institutional and federal oversight roles.
- Triggered by H5N1 transmissibility papers (Imai 2012, Herfst 2012).

**HHS DURC Policy (2012)**
- US Department of Health and Human Services. (2012). *United States Government Policy for Oversight of Life Sciences Dual Use Research of Concern.* HHS.
- Applies to research conducted, funded, or supported by HHS agencies.
- Status: reportedly paused May 2025 ⚠️

**HHS P3CO Framework (2017)**
- US Department of Health and Human Services. (2017). *Framework for Guiding Funding Decisions about Proposed Research Involving Enhanced Potential Pandemic Pathogens.* HHS.
- Creates review process for research reasonably anticipated to create, transfer, or use ePPPs.
- Status: reportedly paused May 2025 ⚠️

## NSABB Case Precedents

**H5N1 Moratorium Cases (2011–2012)**
- NSABB (2012). *Recommendations of the NSABB Regarding the Communication and Publication of the H5N1 Research: NSABB Recommendations.* — Initial recommendation to not publish; reversed after additional NSABB review.
- Casebook cases directly referencing: ENH-01, ECO-03, SCR-02.

**1918 Influenza Reconstruction (2004–2005)**
- NSABB discussions surrounding the Taubenberger reconstruction of 1918 H1N1 sequences.
- Casebook case: SYN-05.

**NSABB 2023 Recommendations**
- NSABB. (2023). *Recommendations for the Evaluation and Oversight of Proposed Gain-of-Function Research.*
- Most recent pre-pause NSABB guidance; relevant to SCR-01 (regulatory pause context) and ENH-01 (GoF evaluation criteria).

## Key NSABB Principles Relevant to Casebook

1. **Institutional responsibility first:** DURC review begins at the institutional level; federal oversight (NSABB) is for the highest-risk cases. Most casebook cases are institutional-level decisions, not NSABB review cases.

2. **Communication ≠ research:** DURC policy applies to research; communicating or explaining published DURC-adjacent science does not automatically trigger DURC obligations. Casebook cases ENH-01, ENH-02, SYN-05 test this boundary.

3. **Intent vs. capability:** DURC categories are defined by what research could produce (capability), not solely by researcher intent. This is why legitimate surveillance work (ENH-03) doesn't trigger DURC even though its subject matter overlaps with Category 1 concerns.

4. **Journal editor role:** NSABB explicitly invests journal editors with responsibility in the DURC communication ecosystem. SCR-02 documents this role.
