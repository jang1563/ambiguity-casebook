## Key 2024–April 2026 Developments

**1. DURC/PEPP Policy Paradigm Shift**
On May 6, 2024, the White House released a unified DURC/PEPP Policy consolidating three decades of fragmented oversight (2012 Federal DURC, 2014 Institutional DURC, 2017 P3CO). Scope expanded from ~15 pathogens to 91 agents and toxins — all Select Agents, Risk Group 3/4, plus future additions. However, on May 5, 2025, Executive Order 14292 paused implementation and directed OSTP to revise within 120 days, creating a ~4-month policy vacuum in federal research governance. Institutions scrambled: NIH rescinded its May 2025 implementation guidance (NOT-OD-25-061) on May 7, 2025; many universities halted new DURC review committees pending clarity.

**2. Nucleic Acid Synthesis Screening Acceleration**
The April 2024 HHS/OSTP Framework for Nucleic Acid Synthesis Screening formalized a three-year transition: screen at 200bp (current), descend to 100bp (2025), reach 50bp by October 2026. This reflects industry consensus (IGSC v3.0, Sept 2024) that smaller fragments enable rapid pathogen reconstruction. IGSC providers now screen against all Select Agents, Australia Group agents, and RG3/4 organisms. However, the May 2025 Executive Order paused this timeline, creating supply-chain confusion. SecureDNA, IBBIS, and cloud-based screening platforms report 2024–2025 adoption challenges among smaller providers and academic synthesis labs.

**3. Australia Group 2024–2025 Modernization**
The 37th Australia Group Plenary (Paris, June 2024) finalized control list updates effective Dec 23, 2024 (Federal Register). The 40th Anniversary Plenary (Sydney, July 2025) expanded to 42 countries with Albania's accession (May 2025). AG now explicitly lists COVID-19/SARS-CoV-2 variants and monkeypox (mpox) as controlled agents, and added AI-driven gene synthesis platforms to dual-use biological equipment controls — the first major regulatory mention of AI-to-biology convergence in export control.

**4. AI Biosecurity Governance Emergence**
Executive Order 14110 (Oct 2023) directed AI biosecurity risk assessment but was rescinded Jan 20, 2025. However, UK AISI's end-to-end biosecurity red-teaming with OpenAI and Anthropic (2024–2025) revealed dozens of jailbreak paths allowing language models to suggest pathogenic sequences or GoF protocols. Seoul Framework commitments (Nov 2024) from 20 AI firms include internal biosafety expert groups and collaboration with regulatory bodies. NIST AI RMF (v1.0 launched 2023; expanded 2024–2025) now includes generative AI and biological design tool profiles. The EU Biotech Act (Dec 2025 proposal, realistic adoption 2027) mandates AI audit trails for synthetic biology designs.

**5. Gene Synthesis Screening Regulatory Harmonization**
IGSC v3.0 (Sept 2024) codified a baseline: screen all sequences encoding toxins, viruses on Australia Group lists, and pathogenicity-enhancing genes from Select Agent bacteria/fungi. HHS 2023 Screening Framework Guidance (updated April 2024) aligned with IGSC. The UK Department of Science, Innovation and Technology (2024–2025) published complementary guidance. However, benchtop nucleic acid synthesis equipment (e.g., Cambrian Genomics, other tabletop synthesizers) remains poorly regulated — only the 2024–2025 EU and UK frameworks explicitly require built-in screening algorithms.

**6. BWC Verification and Biosecurity Mechanisms**
The BWC Working Group's Fifth Session (Dec 2024) faced deadlock: Russia and China opposed verification protocols. The Sixth Session (Aug 2025) produced modest consensus on an Article X International Cooperation and Assistance mechanism. The Seventh Session (Dec 2025) and Eighth Extraordinary Session (Feb 2026) shifted focus toward science & technology assessment frameworks, setting stage for the 10th Review Conference (no later than 2027) to address AI/synthetic biology convergence.

**7. Gain-of-Function Research Pause and Executive Action**
After years of controversy over H5N1 GoF research, Yoshihiro Kawaoka's 2014-paused ferret respiratory-droplet transmissibility study received conditional approval in 2024 with enhanced notification requirements. However, Executive Order 14292 (May 2025) imposed a broad federal funding moratorium on "dangerous gain-of-function research" and prohibited funding for research in Countries of Concern. This triggered international concern: Chinese labs paused collaboration; Iranian researchers faced sanctions threats. The 120-day policy revision window leaves the boundaries of "dangerous" and acceptable GoF research in flux.

## Three-to-Five Casebook Examples Anchored in the Regulatory Landscape

### Case 1: H5N1 Gain-of-Transmission Research (Kawaoka Lab, 2024–2025)
- **Regulatory anchor:** NSABB/DURC (Category 1) + Executive Order 14292 moratorium
- **Scenario:** NIH-funded ferret transmissibility study approved 2024 with enhanced real-time reporting to NIAID. On May 5, 2025, federal funding suspended pending new policy. Lab notified within 48 hours; ongoing work halted.
- **Decision points:** Does new policy permit conditional funding with external oversight? What defines "dangerous" vs. acceptable GoF? Which Countries of Concern are eligible for collaboration in revised policy?
- **Regulatory tension:** Old DURC (Category 1) approved; new EO forbids it; intermediary (May 2024–May 2025) created a dangerous ambiguity in federal governance.

### Case 2: Benchtop Nucleic Acid Synthesis in Academic Lab (2024–2026)
- **Regulatory anchor:** HHS/OSTP Framework (50bp October 2026 deadline) + EU Biotech Act proposal (benchtop equipment must have built-in screening)
- **Scenario:** University synthetic biology lab orders a Cambrian Genomics or similar benchtop synthesizer in 2025. No US law yet mandates internal screening. EU proposal (Dec 2025) would require it; US framework does not. Lab synthesizes 80bp fragment of monkeypox coat protein for diagnostic assay development.
- **Decision points:** Is the 80bp synthesis within scope of current 200bp screening threshold? Is internal screening required? If equipment has no screening, is the provider liable?
- **Regulatory gap:** May 2025 policy pause means benchtop synthesis remains unregulated domestically, though Australia Group and IGSC now list such equipment as controlled dual-use items for export.

### Case 3: International Gene Synthesis Customer Vetting (2024–2025)
- **Regulatory anchor:** IGSC Harmonized Screening Protocol v3.0 + Australia Group export control + potential US/EU customer sanctions
- **Scenario:** A researcher at an Iranian university submits a legitimate order for a 300bp synthetic construct targeting a non-pathogenic gene to an IGSC-member provider. IGSC v3.0 requires sanctions screening; provider conducts KYC and checks against OFAC/EU sanctions lists. Customer is not sanctioned but is affiliated with a university on US entity list.
- **Decision points:** Does IGSC member deny the order under sanctions law? If no explicit sanction, is refusal justified by dual-use principle? What is the provider's liability?
- **Regulatory trend:** Australia Group 2025 Plenary reinforced that providers must conduct beneficial ownership vetting; IGSC v3.0 now mandates it. Liability remains provider-side, creating asymmetric risk.

### Case 4: AI Model Training on Pathogenic Sequences (2024–2025)
- **Regulatory anchor:** Executive Order 14110 (rescinded, but biosecurity provisions lived on in NIST RMF + UK/US AISI agreements) + EU AI Act Reg. 2024/1689
- **Scenario:** A startup trains an LLM on publicly available viral genomic sequences (NCBI, UniProt, COVID-19 open science data) to accelerate protein design. The LLM learns to suggest gain-of-transmissibility mutations if prompted. Red-teaming by UK AISI discovers the jailbreak.
- **Decision points:** Was training data selection a DURC-like decision? Does the startup owe institutional review? Should the model be restricted under EAR?
- **Regulatory gap:** No US law currently governs AI training on biological data for pandemic-potential applications. EU AI Act (2024) only touches downstream use, not model training.

### Case 5: Horsepox Vaccine Development & Synthesis Governance
- **Regulatory anchor:** IGSC v3.0 + FSAP + Australia Group + DURC/PEPP (Category 2 if using synthesized pathogen)
- **Scenario:** Tonix Pharmaceuticals advances TNX-801 (synthesized horsepox-based mpox vaccine) toward Phase 2 trials. Synthetic fragments sourced through IGSC-compliant providers. Fragments flagged but approved for legitimate vaccine use. Institutional IRE reviews pathogen work.
- **Decision points:** What regulatory oversight applies to vaccine development via synthesis vs. traditional culture isolation? Is the vaccine maker subject to FSAP (agent possession)?
- **Regulatory evolution:** 2017 horsepox synthesis spurred policy creation (IGSC 2018, HHS 2023 Framework, IGSC v3.0 2024); now similar research faces more scrutiny but also more defined pathways.

## Summary

As of April 2026, dual-use biology governance is at an inflection point. The May 2024 DURC/PEPP consolidation promised unified federal oversight; the May 2025 Executive Order paused it. Gene synthesis screening accelerates (50bp by Oct 2026 in theory) but the GoF moratorium and Country-of-Concern funding restrictions have introduced geopolitical friction. Australia Group and IGSC now include AI-driven platforms and benchtop synthesis equipment in scope — the first regulatory acknowledgment of AI-to-biology convergence. The EU Biotech Act (Dec 2025 proposal) offers the most comprehensive synthesis-focused framework but remains 1–2 years from adoption. For the Casebook, focus on the regulatory *ambiguity* created by the May 2025 policy pause, the tension between synthesis innovation and screening burden, and the emerging AI-bio convergence that existing frameworks (DURC, FSAP, Australia Group) were not designed to address.
